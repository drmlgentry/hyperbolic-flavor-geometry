Why disjoint support forces disjoint fields

Suppose two of these Galois extensions shared a nontrivial common subfield F ⊆ ℚ. F would have to be unramified at every prime dividing either extension's discriminant — and since the two supports share no prime at all, F is unramified everywhere.

Minkowski's theorem: the only extension of ℚ unramified at every prime is ℚ itself.

So F = ℚ — the fields are automatically linearly disjoint, with no closure of the full compositum needed.
