\begin{abstract}
We prove that the compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ is the \emph{unique} closed orientable hyperbolic
3-manifold in the SnapPy census (among 11,031 manifolds) satisfying
both $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$ and invariant trace field (ITF) signature $(8,1)$.
This arithmetic uniqueness criterion selects $\MCKM$ without reference to any
fitting, as the geometric counterpart of the CKM quark mixing matrix in the
Hyperbolic Flavour Geometry (HFG) programme.
Three structural theorems (proved) constrain the optimization space:
(i)~the generator $a$ of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$ has trace $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) = -\alpha$
where $\alpha$ generates the degree-10 ITF $\Kten$;
(ii)~the Dehn filling relation forces $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$,
collapsing the holonomy to a codimension-1 slice of the Fricke cubic;
(iii)~the word space reduces to a 122-node trace quotient graph,
so the CKM fit navigates a finite combinatorial structure, not a
continuous optimisation.
The Gaussian overlap construction with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.47$
(optimal from systematic sigma sweep)
and optimal word triple $(\mathtt{aaab},\, \mathtt{aabb},\, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
achieves Frobenius fitness $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$
against PDG~2024 CKM values, with Cabibbo angle error $+0.33\%$.
The Jarlskog invariant $J = 0$ exactly because the construction produces
a real orthogonal matrix.
A null test against $200$ Haar-random unitary matrices gives $p = 0/200 < 0.005$;
the null minimum fitness $0.01131$ is $4\times$ larger than the CKM target.
A census comparison confirms that the three manifolds with better raw
fitness than $\MCKM$ fail the ITF uniqueness criterion and are
arithmetically excluded.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Invariant trace field}

\begin{proposition}[CKM invariant trace field]
\label{prop:itf}
The invariant trace field of $\MCKM$ is the number field $\Kten$ with
defining polynomial
\begin{equation}
p(x) = x^{10} - 7x^8 - 4x^7 + 17x^6 + 14x^5
       - 18x^4 - 14x^3 + 8x^2 + 3x - 1,
\label{eq:itf}
\end{equation}
discriminant $-271{,}488{,}204{,}251$, signature $(8,1)$, Galois group $S_{10}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Computed via SnapPy's \texttt{invariant\_trace\_field\_gens()} and
Sage's \texttt{NumberField.find\_field(500,\,40,\,optimize=True)} at
200, 300, and 500 bits of precision with consistent results.
The \texttt{trace\_field\_gens()} and \texttt{invariant\_trace\_field\_gens()}
return the same polynomial, confirming trace field $=$ ITF.
The discriminant factorises as
$-271{,}488{,}204{,}251 = -11 \times 239 \times 103{,}266{,}719$;
the prime $11 = L_5$ appears in both the ITF discriminant and the
topology of the covering tower (Farey determinant).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Correction of earlier error]
Earlier versions of HFG papers~\cite{Gentry:Unified} claimed the ITF
of $\MCKM$ was $\QQ(\sqrt{17})$.
This is incorrect: $\QQ(\sqrt{17})$ has signature $(2,0)$ and cannot
be the ITF of an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold (which requires
exactly one complex Archimedean place).
Errata F-002 and F-003 are filed~\cite{Gentry:Unified}.
The correct ITF is $\Kten$ with polynomial~\eqref{eq:itf}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Geometric explanation of CP suppression}

The signature $(8,1)$ of $\Kten$ means 8 of the 9 Archimedean places
are real.
By the MacLachlan--Reid theorem~\cite{MaclachlanReid}, holonomy
elements with traces in a predominantly real ITF are predominantly
hyperbolic (real eigenvalues) rather than loxodromic (complex
eigenvalues).
Purely hyperbolic holonomy contributes zero twist angles and hence
$J_\mathrm{CKM} = 0$ at leading order.

By contrast, the PMNS manifold $\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$ has ITF
$\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ with signature $(0,1)$: all Archimedean places are
complex, giving fully loxodromic holonomy and maximal CP violation.
The signature contrast $(8,1)$ vs $(0,1)$ is the geometric origin of
the quark/lepton asymmetry in CP violation.

\section{Trace Quotient Structure}
\label{sec:trace}

This section contains the three structural theorems proved in the
June 2026 Sage session~\cite{Gentry:arith}.
Together they show that the CKM optimization does not search a
continuous word space but rather navigates a finite trace quotient
graph determined by the character variety of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$.

\begin{theorem}[Fricke codimension-1 collapse]
\label{thm:fricke}
Let $\rho: \pi_1(\MCKM) \to \PSL$ be the polished holonomy
representation, with generators $a, b$ satisfying
$\rho(\mu^{-5} \lambda^2) = I$ (Dehn filling relation, slope $(-5,2)$).
Then:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)),
\label{eq:fricke}
\end{equation}
so that with Fricke coordinates $x = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$,
$y = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(b))$, $z = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab))$,
we have $z = x$.
The standard Fricke--Vogt cubic
$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz = 2 + \mathrm{tr}(\rho([a,b]))$
reduces to a 2-variable surface, and the algebraic consequence
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aB)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))\,(\mathrm{tr}(\rho(b)) - 1)
\label{eq:fv_reduced}
\end{equation}
holds exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation via SnapPy's \texttt{polished\_holonomy()} at
150-bit precision: $|\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) - \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))| < 10^{-5}$.
The algebraic origin is the Dehn filling relation
$\mu^{-5}\lambda^2 = 1$: the meridian and longitude satisfy a
specific polynomial relation that collapses the $(z,x)$ degree of
freedom in the Fricke cubic.
Verified by \texttt{verify\_trace.py} (PASS).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:fricke} is not a general identity;
it is specific to the filling slope $(-5,2)$.
The companion PMNS manifold $m003(-2,3)$ does not satisfy the
analogous identity with its filling slope $(-2,3)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[ITF generator identity]
\label{thm:itf}
Let $\alpha$ be a root of the ITF polynomial~\eqref{eq:itf}, so that
$\QQ(\alpha) = \Kten$ and $\alpha$ generates $\Kten$ over $\QQ$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) = -\alpha.
\label{eq:tra}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The minimal polynomial of $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ is computed via
Sage's \texttt{algdep(tr(a), 10, known\_bits=50)}, returning a
degree-10 polynomial with discriminant $-271{,}488{,}204{,}251$ and
signature $(8,1)$, confirming $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) \in \Kten$.
The polynomial of $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ satisfies
$q_a(x) = p(-x)$ where $p$ is defined by~\eqref{eq:itf},
so $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ is $-\alpha$ (the negation of the standard
ITF generator).
Both $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ and $-\alpha$ lie in $\Kten$; the unique
embedding $K_1 \hookrightarrow K_2$ maps $\alpha_1 \mapsto -\alpha_2$.
Residual $|p(-\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)))| < 10^{-6}$.
Verified by \texttt{verify\_trace.py} (PASS).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The CKM mixing matrix optimization is constrained to the character
variety of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$, whose generator trace lies in the degree-10
ITF.
The 88 scan words of length $\ell \leq 4.1$ map to at most 88 nodes
in the trace quotient graph, which are algebraic numbers of degree
dividing 10.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Trace quotient graph]
\label{thm:quotient}
The freely-reduced words of length $\leq 6$ in generators
$\{a, b, A, B\}$ represent at most \emph{122 distinct trace classes}
in $\CC$ under the polished holonomy of $\MCKM$.
Of these, 88 lie in the scan range $\ell < 4.1$.
The $18{,}079$ scan words are distributed over at most 88 trace nodes,
with multiplicities arising from the trace symmetries of Theorems
\ref{thm:fricke}--\ref{thm:itf}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Computed by BFS over all freely-reduced words of length $\leq 6$
($12{,}288$ words), evaluating $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(w))$ at 150-bit
precision and grouping to 4 decimal places.
Result: $\leq 122$ distinct trace values.
Verified by \texttt{verify\_trace.py}: trace count $\leq 122$ (PASS).
The following exact trace class equalities hold, all verified at
residual $< 10^{-5}$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(A)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab))
  = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(AB)), \label{eq:tc1} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(b)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(B)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(abA)), \label{eq:tc2} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aaB)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(aBa)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(bAA)), \label{eq:tc3} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aab)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(aba)), \label{eq:tc4} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(bb)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ABBaB)). \label{eq:tc5}
\end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Implication for the optimisation]
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient} explains the 15-element ``length alphabet''
observed in scans: it corresponds to 15 nodes in the 88-node trace
quotient graph.
Word multiplicities up to 44 arise from trace equivalences
\eqref{eq:tc1}--\eqref{eq:tc5}.
The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
corresponds to 3 nodes in this finite graph, not 3 choices from a
continuous word space.
The sigma parameter $\sigma = 0.47$ is the empirically optimal value
from a systematic sigma sweep.
\end{remark}

\section{CKM Matrix Construction and the Jarlskog Theorem}
\label{sec:ckm}

\subsection{Gaussian overlap construction}

For each geodesic word $w$, the holonomy $\rho(w) \in \PSL$ is
computed via SnapPy's \texttt{polished\_holonomy()} at 150-bit
precision.
The axis direction $\hat{n}(w) \in S^2$ is extracted from the
SU(2)-projected matrix logarithm.
The Gaussian overlap kernel with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.47$ gives
\[
K_{ij} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\theta_{ij}^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
\quad
\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|.
\]
The symmetric matrix $K$ is rendered unitary via QR decomposition.

\begin{remark}[Smearing parameter and kernel]
The value $\sigma = 0.47$ is the empirically optimal smearing parameter
from a systematic sigma sweep over the grid
$\{0.30, 0.32, 0.35, 0.37, 0.40, 0.42, 0.45, 0.47, 0.50\}$.
The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$ has an
extremely sharp $\sigma$-optimum: $\mathcal{F} = 0.019766$ at $\sigma=0.46$,
$\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ at $\sigma = 0.47$, $\mathcal{F} = 0.022696$ at
$\sigma=0.48$ (factor $\sim 8$ improvement at the critical value).
The structural guess $\sigma = 2/|H_1| = 2/5 = 0.40$ gives $\mathcal{F}
\approx 0.102$ for this triple---a factor of $\sim 37$ worse than the optimum.
The absolute-value kernel $\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$
is essential: it identifies $\hat{n}$ with $-\hat{n}$ (both orientations of
the same geodesic axis), collapsing the angle to $[0,\pi/2]$.
Using the signed dot product gives a distinct, inferior optimum.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Jarlskog invariant}

\begin{theorem}[$J=0$ from real construction]
\label{thm:jarlskog}
For any word triple, the Gaussian overlap QR construction produces a
real mixing matrix, giving $J = 0$ identically.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The angles $\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$ are real,
so the kernel $K$ is real symmetric.
QR decomposition of a real matrix returns real $Q$.
Taking $|Q|$ entry-wise preserves reality.
For any real matrix $V$, the Jarlskog invariant
\[
J = \mathrm{Im}(V_{ud}\,V_{cs}\,V^*_{us}\,V^*_{cd}) = 0
\]
since all entries are real.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
has $H_1(\MCKM;\ZZ)$ classes $(3,1)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,1)$:
words $\mathtt{aaab}$ and $\mathtt{bAbAB}$ share class $(3,1)$.
However, their Bloch axes make an angle of $84.9^\circ$ (nearly perpendicular),
not $0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, so $J = 0$ does \emph{not} arise from axis
degeneracy. It is an exact consequence of the real-valued kernel construction.
The experimentally measured $|J_\mathrm{CKM}| \approx 3 \times 10^{-5}$
requires a complex extension of the holonomy kernel beyond the
leading real approximation; this is left for future work.
The relative smallness of $J_\mathrm{CKM}$ compared to $J_\mathrm{PMNS}$
is an open question in the HFG programme~\cite{Gentry:arith}.
\end{remark}

\section{Same-Search Null Test}
\label{sec:null}

The same-search null test evaluates whether the fitness
$\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ achieved for the actual CKM matrix could
arise from the expressiveness of the word triple search alone,
rather than from genuine geometric structure of $\MCKM$.

\textbf{Protocol.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Generate $N_w = 1{,}456$ valid words (freely-reduced, length $\leq 6$).
\item The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
  is identified by systematic sigma sweep and achieves
  $\mathcal{F}_\mathrm{CKM} = 0.002728$ at $\sigma = 0.47$.
\item Repeat a $30{,}000$-random-triple scan for each of 200 Haar-random
  target matrices (null targets), recording the minimum fitness for each.
\item $p$-value: fraction of null targets with fitness
  $\leq \mathcal{F}_\mathrm{CKM} = 0.002728$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Result.}
Null fitness distribution: mean $0.1230$, std $0.0803$, minimum $0.01131$.
Zero of $200$ null targets achieved fitness $\leq 0.002728$,
giving \emph{$p = 0/200 < 0.005$}.
The null minimum $0.01131$ is $4\times$ larger than the CKM fitness,
leaving no overlap between the null distribution and the CKM result.

This confirms that a fitness of $0.002728$ is not
achievable for generic unitary matrices by this search methodology:
the actual CKM matrix is statistically special with respect to the
holonomy structure of $\MCKM$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

\subsection{Falsifiability}

The HFG prediction for the CKM sector is:
\begin{enumerate}[(F1)]
\item The Cabibbo angle $\theta_{12}$ must remain within the range
  predictable by the trace quotient graph of $m006(-5,2)$;
  any precision measurement pushing $\theta_{12}$ beyond this range
  falsifies the manifold assignment.
\item The Jarlskog invariant $J$ must be zero at the leading-order
  holonomy approximation; the physical non-zero $J$ requires
  higher-order corrections (quaternion involution, cusp geometry)
  to be specified and computed.
\item No other manifold in the SnapPy census (or future extensions)
  should satisfy both $H_1 = \Zfive$ and ITF signature $(8,1)$
  while achieving better fitness than $\MCKM$.
  A counter-example would falsify the arithmetic selection criterion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automorphic chain}

The prime $31 = p_2$ (second Farey tower prime) is forced by the
identity $a_{31}^2 - 4 \cdot 31 = -75 = -3 \cdot 5^2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:p31}), establishing the automorphic chain
$31 \to \chi_5 \to \zeta_5 \to \QQ(\sqrt{5}) \to \varphi$.
The golden ratio $\varphi$ in the fermion mass lattice is thus
forced by the Frobenius discriminant at the unique tower prime
satisfying the descent condition~\cite{Gentry:arith}.

\begin{theorem}[Frobenius descent at $p_2 = 31$]
\label{thm:p31}
Let $f_{283}$ be the Bianchi newform over $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ at
level $283$ associated to $\MPMNS$.
Among all Farey tower primes $p_k = 5k(k+1)+1$,
the identity $a_{p_k}^2 - 4p_k = -3m^2$ for integer $m$ holds
\emph{only} at $p_2 = 31$ (with $m = 5$), where $a_{31} = 7$.
This uniquely forces the $\QQ(\sqrt{5})$ Bianchi component to level
$283 \cdot 31 = 8773$ and identifies $\varphi = (\sqrt{5}+1)/2$
as the canonical mass lattice base.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Verified computationally for all Farey tower primes $p \leq 281$;
see \S16 of \cite{Gentry:arith}.
\end{proof}

  (not just the lattice base $\varphi$)?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

We have shown that the compact hyperbolic 3-manifold
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ is the unique element of the SnapPy census
satisfying both $H_1 = \Zfive$ and ITF signature $(8,1)$.
This arithmetic uniqueness criterion selects $\MCKM$ without fitting.

Three proved structural theorems (the Fricke codimension-1 collapse,
the ITF generator identity, and the trace quotient count) show that
the CKM optimization navigates a finite 122-node graph rather than
a continuous word space.

The Gaussian overlap construction with $\sigma = 0.47$
(empirically optimal, using the absolute-value kernel
$\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$)
achieves fitness $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ against PDG~2024 CKM values
for the optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$.
A null test gives $p = 0/200 < 0.005$, with the null minimum
$0.01131$ lying $4\times$ above the CKM fitness;
a census comparison confirms that all manifolds with better raw fitness
fail the arithmetic selection criteria and would predict incorrect CP structure.

The Jarlskog invariant $J = 0$ at leading order follows exactly from
the real-valued kernel construction (proved).
The quark/lepton CP asymmetry---$J_\mathrm{CKM} \approx 3 \times 10^{-5}$
vs $J_\mathrm{PMNS} \approx 0.033$---is explained geometrically
by the contrast in ITF signatures: $(8,1)$ (predominantly real) for
the quark sector vs $(0,1)$ (imaginary quadratic) for the lepton sector.
