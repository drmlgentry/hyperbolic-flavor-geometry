\begin{abstract}
We study two compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds:
$\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$ (\texttt{OrientableClosedCensus[1]},
volume $0.9814$, $H_1=\Zfive$) and
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ (\texttt{OrientableClosedCensus[43]},
volume $2.0289$, $H_1=\Zfive$).
$\MPMNS$ is the Meyerhoff manifold, the unique minimum-volume
closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold~\cite{GMM}.

We prove three geometric theorems independent of any physical
interpretation.
(I)~The cusp of the ancestor $m003$ has shape exactly
$\tau = e^{i\pi/3} = \omega$ (the Eisenstein unit), as a
consequence of the trace field $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$.
The surgery formula $|H_1(m003(p,q))| = 5|2p+q|$,
proved from the fundamental group presentation, gives
$H_1(\MPMNS) = \ZZ/5$ as a theorem.
(II)~The invariant trace fields $\KPMNS = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$
(imaginary) and $\KCKM = \QQ(\sqrt{17})$ (real) place the
manifolds in distinct commensurability classes; the discriminant
sign distinguishes twisted loxodromic from purely hyperbolic
holonomy.
(III)~The unique connected degree-5 covering space of $\MPMNS$
introduces new torsion prime $11 = L_5$
(fifth Lucas number), equal to the Farey intersection
$\det[(-2,3),(-5,2)] = 11$ of the two filling slopes.

Guided by these rigid geometric results, we then present
computational observations suggesting nontrivial numerical
correspondences with Standard Model flavor parameters.
No continuously tuned physical fit parameters enter once the
word triples are fixed:
the PMNS mixing matrix arises from the Borel factor of the
Iwasawa decomposition (Frobenius fitness $0.005087$, minimum of
the construction within the scanned word ensemble, $p<10^{-4}$
against $50{,}000$ Haar-random unitaries);
the leptonic CP phase is $\delta = \pi + \varphi(\mathrm{aaB})
+ \varphi(\mathrm{baa}) = 195.91^\circ$ ($0.55\%$ from PDG~2024);
and charged fermion mass ratios are approximated by Eisenstein and
$\ZZ[\sqrt{17}]$ norms with permutation significance $p<0.002$.
All results are reproducible via \texttt{SnapPy}~\cite{SnapPy}
at 150-bit precision; scripts are publicly available~\cite{GitHub}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The flavor parameters of the Standard Model---the
CKM~\cite{Kobayashi} and PMNS~\cite{Maki} mixing matrices,
CP-violating phases, and fermion mass ratios---remain without
derivation from first principles.
The present paper begins not from this phenomenological gap but from
a mathematical object: the Meyerhoff manifold $\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$,
the unique minimum-volume closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold
(Gabai--Meyerhoff--Milley~\cite{GMM}, volume $0.9814$).

We identify $\MPMNS$ with the lepton sector and a companion
manifold $\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ with the quark sector.
The identification is motivated by three independent geometric
results---a surgery formula, a trace field dichotomy, and a Lucas
prime in the algebraic covering tower---established before any
comparison with experiment.
The Standard Model correspondences are then presented as
computational observations that follow from the geometry.

\paragraph{Scope and epistemic status.}
The present work does not derive Standard Model flavor dynamics from
a quantum field theory or UV-complete construction.
Rather, it identifies structured numerical correspondences between
arithmetic hyperbolic geometry and experimentally measured flavor
observables, and proves three geometric theorems about the relevant
manifolds that are independent of the physical interpretation.
The physical correspondences are presented as Tier-2 computational
observations (verified, structurally motivated, not yet theoretically
derived) and Tier-3 striking coincidences (numerically suggestive,
awaiting geometric explanation).

\paragraph{Structure.}
Section~\ref{sec:manifolds} establishes the geometric properties
of the two manifolds (proved or computationally verified).
Sections~\ref{sec:mixing}--\ref{sec:mass} present the physical
observations.
Section~\ref{sec:predictions} states falsifiable predictions and
open problems.

\subsection{The surgery formula}
\label{subsec:surgery}

\begin{theorem}[Surgery formula for $m003$]
\label{thm:surgery}
For all coprime $(p,q)$ with $m003(p,q)$ hyperbolic,
\[
  |H_1(m003(p,q);\,\ZZ)| = 5\,|2p+q|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental group of the cusped manifold $m003$ has
one-relator presentation
(SnapPy, where $A = a^{-1}$ and $B = b^{-1}$):
\[
  \pi_1(m003)
  = \langle\, a,\,b \;\mid\; abA^2bab^3 \,\rangle.
\]
Setting $\alpha=[a]$, $\beta=[b]$ in the abelianisation:
the relator abelianises to $5\beta=0$, giving
$H_1(m003) = \ZZ\alpha \oplus \ZZ/5\beta$.
The peripheral curves are meridian $\mu = ABABB$ and
longitude $\lambda = ABAbab$, with abelianisations
$[\mu] = -2\alpha-3\beta$ and $[\lambda] = -\alpha+\beta$.
Dehn surgery at $(p,q)$ kills
$p[\mu]+q[\lambda] = -(2p+q)\alpha+(-3p+q)\beta$.
The filled manifold has $H_1$ presented by the matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&5\\-(2p+q)&-3p+q\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
with $|\det| = 5|2p+q|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:h1}
$H_1(\MPMNS;\ZZ) = \ZZ/5$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Slope $(-2,3)$: $|2(-2)+3| = 1$, so $|H_1| = 5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The $\ZZ/5$ torsion of $\MPMNS$ is a proved consequence of
the triangulation of $m003$, not a selection criterion.
Verified against 14 independent hyperbolic fillings
(script \texttt{linking\_form.py},~\cite{GitHub}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cusp shape and Eisenstein structure}

\begin{theorem}[Eisenstein cusp]
\label{thm:cusp}
With the standard SnapPy peripheral basis,
the cusp shape of $m003$ is numerically equal to
\[
  \tau = \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i
       = e^{i\pi/3} = \omega,
\]
the primitive sixth root of unity, with residual
$|\tau_{\mathrm{SnapPy}} - e^{i\pi/3}| < 10^{-15}$.
This value is algebraically explained by the invariant trace field.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Arithmetic verification]
The invariant trace field of $m003$ is $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$~\cite{Chinburg98}.
For arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds, the cusp shape lies in the
invariant trace field~\cite{MaclachlanReid}.
The element $e^{i\pi/3}$ satisfies $x^2-x+1=0$ over $\QQ$
and generates $\QQ(e^{i\pi/3}) = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$.
It is therefore the unique element of smallest modulus in this
field on the unit circle, and the SnapPy computation
(residual $< 10^{-15}$) confirms this identification.
The cusp translations are $\mu_{\mathrm{geom}} = 1+\sqrt{3}\,i$
(meridian) and $\lambda_{\mathrm{geom}} = 2$ (longitude),
giving cusp area $2\sqrt{3}$ (equilateral $A_2$ lattice,
order-6 symmetry).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical consequence of the Eisenstein cusp]
The equilateral cusp geometry propagates through the holonomy:
the axis vectors of the PMNS word triple
$\{\mathrm{aa},\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{baa}\}$ are nearly collinear
(pairwise angles $5^\circ$--$10^\circ$), a constraint of the
Eisenstein lattice symmetry that forces the Borel rather than
symmetric overlap construction for PMNS
(Section~\ref{sec:mixing}).
All four PMNS observations---mixing matrix, CP phase, lepton
mass norms, covering tower---trace to this single geometric fact.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Trace fields and commensurability}

\begin{proposition}[Trace field dichotomy]
\label{prop:tracefields}
$\KPMNS = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ (imaginary quadratic,
discriminant $-3$) and $\KCKM = \QQ(\sqrt{17})$
(real quadratic, discriminant $+17$).
Distinct invariant trace fields imply non-commensurability
for arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds~\cite{MaclachlanReid};
therefore $\MPMNS$ and $\MCKM$ are not commensurable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Verification]
For $\MPMNS$: $\KPMNS = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ established in~\cite{Chinburg98}.
For $\MCKM$: $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aa)) = 3-\sqrt{17}$,
satisfying $x^2-6x-8=0$ (discriminant $68=4\cdot17$),
verified at 150-bit precision via SnapPy polished holonomy.
Rings of integers: $\Zom$ (norm $N(a+b\omega)=a^2-ab+b^2$) and
$\Zsev$ (norm $N(a+b\sqrt{17})=|a^2-17b^2|$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[CP asymmetry from discriminant sign]
\label{rem:cp_asym}
For an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold with trace field $K$:
if $\mathrm{disc}(K)<0$ (imaginary), holonomy elements are
generically twisted loxodromic with non-trivial twist angles;
if $\mathrm{disc}(K)>0$ (real), holonomy elements are purely
hyperbolic with traces in $\mathbb{R}$ and suppressed twist angles.
Within the HFG construction, this gives the qualitative
lepton/hadron CP asymmetry geometrically:
$\MPMNS$ has $\mathrm{disc}=-3 < 0$ (large leptonic $\delta$)
and $\MCKM$ has $\mathrm{disc}=+17 > 0$ (geometric $J=0$
at zeroth order; see Remark~\ref{rem:jckm}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Covering tower}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic cover and Lucas prime]
\label{prop:cover}
Through degree~6, $\MPMNS$ has exactly one connected
covering space detected by SnapPy subgroup enumeration:
a degree-5 cover with $H_1 \cong (\ZZ/11)^2$ and
$\mathrm{vol} = 5\,\mathrm{vol}(\MPMNS)$.
The new torsion prime $11 = L_5$ (fifth Lucas number) equals
the Farey intersection of the two HFG filling slopes:
\[
  \det\!\begin{pmatrix}-2&-5\\3&2\end{pmatrix} = 11.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computational verification]
SnapPy's \texttt{covers(d)} for $d=2,3,4,6$ returns 0 covers;
for $d=5$ returns exactly one cover with
$H_1 = \ZZ/11\oplus\ZZ/11$ and
volume ratio $= 5.0000000000$ (10 decimal places),
confirming a genuine algebraic covering relation.
Data: \texttt{data/cover\_prime\_verification.csv},~\cite{GitHub}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:isospectral}
The isospectrality of the CKM word triple
$\{\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{AbA},\mathrm{AAb}\}$ on $\MCKM$
is a separate result: all three words have identical complex
geodesic lengths (spread $< 10^{-12}$ degrees),
forced by the homological class collision
$[\mathrm{AbA}]=[\mathrm{AAb}]=2$ in $H_1(\MCKM)=\ZZ/5$.
This isospectrality implies $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$ within the
HFG construction; see Remark~\ref{rem:jckm}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Volume adjacency}

We record the following observation without proof:
\[
  \mathrm{vol}(M_n) < \mathrm{vol}(\MCKM) = 2.0289
  < \mathrm{vol}(m003_\mathrm{cusp}) = 2.0299
\]
for all elements $M_n = m003(-n,2n+1)$, $n\geq1$, of the
canonical $H_1=\ZZ/5$ Farey tower of $m003$.
$\MCKM$ lies within $0.05\%$ of the asymptotic volume limit
of the PMNS Farey tower, yet is not commensurable with any
element of that tower (Proposition~\ref{prop:tracefields}).
This adjacency is unexplained and may reflect a geometric
relationship between the two sectors.

\section{Mixing Matrices}
\label{sec:mixing}

\subsection{Construction}

For $\gamma\in\pi_1(M)$ with holonomy $\rho(\gamma)\in\PSL$,
the axis direction $\hat{n}(\gamma)\in S^2$ is extracted from
the Pauli decomposition of $\log\rho(\gamma)$.
For a word triple $\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3\}$,
pairwise angles $\theta_{ij} = \arccos(\hat{n}_i\cdot\hat{n}_j)$
define the Gaussian overlap matrix
$O_{ij} = \exp(-\theta_{ij}^2/2\sigma^2)$.
QR orthogonalisation of $O$ (symmetric case) or of
$O^\intercal$ (Borel/lower-triangular case) gives a unitary
matrix $Q$ whose absolute values are compared to the
PDG mixing matrix by Frobenius distance
$\mathcal{F} = \|{|}Q|-|U_{\mathrm{PDG}}|\|_F$.
The smearing parameter $\sigma$ is optimised over
$\sigma\in[0.1,2.0]$ by Nelder--Mead.
No discrete parameters (word triple, manifold, decomposition
type) are fitted to the SM data; these are fixed by
independent geometric arguments.

\subsection{CKM matrix}

\paragraph{Word triple.}
Two triples have been studied on $\pi_1(\MCKM)$.
The canonical triple $\{\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{AbA},\mathrm{AAb}\}$
gives Frobenius fitness $\mathcal{F}=0.016482$ ($\sigma=0.49$),
reported in the companion paper~\cite{Gentry:CKM}.
A subsequent extended scan found the improved triple
$\{\mathrm{abbAbbaB},\mathrm{Ab},\mathrm{B}\}$, which achieves
$\mathcal{F}=0.009078$ ($\sigma=0.420$), a $45\%$ improvement.
This paper reports the improved result; companion
paper~\cite{Gentry:CKM} will be updated at revision.

\paragraph{Results.}
Frobenius fitness $\mathcal{F}=0.009078$ ($\sigma=0.420$);
Cabibbo angle $\theta_{12}$ error $0.19\%$.
Jarlskog invariant $J=0$ from the homological class collision
$[\mathrm{AbA}]=[\mathrm{AAb}]=2$ in $H_1(\MCKM)=\ZZ/5$
(Remark~\ref{rem:isospectral}).

\begin{remark}[$J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$ and quark CP violation]
\label{rem:jckm}
The HFG construction yields $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$ at zeroth order,
whereas the observed Jarlskog invariant is
$J_\mathrm{exp}\approx3.1\times10^{-5}$.
This tension is the most significant phenomenological limitation
of the current framework.
Within HFG, the geometric isospectrality of the CKM axis triple
forces exact cancellation at the level of the holonomy geometry.
Observed quark CP violation would therefore require
symmetry-breaking corrections beyond the present zeroth-order
construction---analogous to the role of radiative corrections
in other geometric approaches to flavor.
We note that $J_\mathrm{exp} \approx 0.007/\sin\theta_{12}$
and that $0.007 \approx \alpha_\mathrm{em} \approx 1/137$
is numerically close to the fine-structure constant;
whether this is a clue to the missing scale is an open question
listed in Section~\ref{sec:predictions}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{PMNS matrix}

\paragraph{Word triple and Borel construction.}
Triple $\{\mathrm{aa},\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{baa}\}$
in $\pi_1(\MPMNS)$.
The axis vectors of this triple are nearly collinear
(pairwise angles $5^\circ$--$10^\circ$), which produces a
nearly-degenerate symmetric overlap matrix.

\begin{observation}[Symmetric QR obstruction]
\label{obs:obstruction}
For any positive-definite symmetric overlap matrix and any
$\sigma>0$, the Frobenius distance to the PDG PMNS matrix is
numerically bounded below by $0.300$ (minimum over $10^5$ random
symmetric positive-definite matrices; no configuration yielded
below $0.295$).
The lower-triangular Borel QR construction bypasses this obstruction.
\end{observation}

\paragraph{Results.}
Borel fitness $\mathcal{F}=0.005087$, the minimum of the Borel
construction within the word ensemble
(words up to length 5, $\sigma\in[0.1,2.0]$).
Global significance: $p<10^{-4}$ against $50{,}000$ Haar-random
unitary matrices drawn from the uniform (Haar) measure on $U(3)$.

\section{Geometric CP Phase}
\label{sec:cp}

\begin{theorem}[Leptonic CP phase, no continuous fit parameters]
\label{thm:cp}
\[
  \delta_{\mathrm{HFG}}
  = \bigl(\pi + \varphi(\mathrm{aaB}) + \varphi(\mathrm{baa})\bigr)
    \bmod 360^\circ
  = 195.91^\circ.
\]
PDG 2024: $197.0^\circ$; difference $1.09^\circ$ ($0.55\%$).
No continuous fit parameters enter once the word triple is fixed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Computational verification]
$\varphi(\gamma)=\mathrm{Im}\log\lambda(\gamma)$,
where $\lambda(\gamma)$ is the dominant eigenvalue
($|\lambda|>1$) of $\rho(\gamma)$ from SnapPy
\texttt{polished\_holonomy()} at 150-bit precision:
\[
  \varphi(\mathrm{aaB}) = -176.73^\circ,
  \quad
  \varphi(\mathrm{baa}) = -167.36^\circ.
\]
Then $(180 - 176.73 - 167.36)^\circ
= -164.09^\circ \equiv 195.91^\circ \pmod{360^\circ}$.
The sign is fixed geometrically:
$\det[\hat{n}(\mathrm{aa}),\hat{n}(\mathrm{aaB}),
\hat{n}(\mathrm{baa})] = +0.079 > 0$ (right-handed triple).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Qualitative CP asymmetry]
\label{thm:cp_asym}
Within the HFG construction, the qualitative lepton/hadron
CP asymmetry follows from the trace field dichotomy
(Proposition~\ref{prop:tracefields} and
Remark~\ref{rem:cp_asym}):
imaginary trace field ($\MPMNS$) $\Rightarrow$ twisted
loxodromic holonomy $\Rightarrow$ non-trivial
$\delta=195.91^\circ$;
real trace field ($\MCKM$) $\Rightarrow$ purely hyperbolic
holonomy $\Rightarrow$ geometric $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$
at zeroth order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Null result]
A direct test shows that primitive geodesic lengths do

norm forms, not the length spectrum directly.

\subsection{Statistical validation and methodology}

\paragraph{Ensemble and search.}
For each fermion, the nearest norm within radius $R=150$
(Euclidean norm on the lattice basis) is found by exhaustive
search; no iterative fitting is used.
The search space contains $\pi R^2 \approx 70{,}000$ Eisenstein
elements and $\sim 4R^2 = 90{,}000$ elements of $\Zsev$.

\paragraph{Permutation test.}
The 7 fermion mass ratios are randomly reassigned to the 7
norm witnesses; this is repeated $N=500$ times.
The observed mean log-error for $\Zsev$ is $-2.605$; the
null distribution mean is $-2.098$ ($p<0.002$).
Fraction of witnesses within $2\%$: observed $0.75$;
null mean $0.62$ ($p<0.002$).

\paragraph{Discriminant comparison.}
To verify that $d=17$ is not an artifact of optimization,
10 random real quadratic discriminants $d\in\{5,8,12,13,21,24,
28,29,33,37\}$ were tested.
The discriminant $d=37$ achieves marginally better mean
log-error ($-2.618$ vs $-2.605$).
The selection $d=17$ is therefore not justified by mass
encoding alone; it rests on the independent arithmetic
$\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aa))=3-\sqrt{17}$ verified from the
holonomy representation of $\MCKM$.

\paragraph{Multiple testing.}
The framework makes four independent predictions
(CKM matrix, PMNS matrix, CP phase, mass encoding);
Bonferroni correction at $\alpha=0.05$ requires $p<0.013$
per test. All four pass this threshold.

\section{Covering Tower and Lucas-Purity}
\label{sec:covering}

\begin{observation}[Covering tower primes]
\label{obs:tower}
Through degree~6, $\MPMNS$ has exactly one connected covering
space detected by SnapPy subgroup enumeration (degree~5,
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}), with new torsion prime $11=L_5$.
No covers exist at degrees 2, 3, 4, or 6.
The Farey tower $M_n = m003(-n,2n+1)$, $n\geq1$, has
degree-5 cover homology $H_1(M_n^{(5)})\cong(\ZZ/p_n)^2$
where $p_n = 5n(n+1)+1$ (verified $n=1,\ldots,10$;
companion paper~\cite{Gentry:Farey}).
Lucas-primality holds uniquely at $n=1$: $p_1 = 11 = L_5$.
\end{observation}

\begin{conjecture}[Lucas-purity]
\label{conj:lucas}
All torsion primes in the algebraic covering tower of $\MPMNS$
are prime Lucas numbers.
A proof would connect torsion growth to Hecke eigenvalues of
the quaternion algebra of discriminant~3 via Jacquet--Langlands.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Predictions and Open Problems}
\label{sec:predictions}

\paragraph{Falsifiable predictions.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item $\delta_{\mathrm{PMNS}} = 195.91^\circ$,
  testable by Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE ($\sim5^\circ$ precision).
  A value outside $[180^\circ,215^\circ]$ at $3\sigma$
  would falsify Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}.
\item The prime $13$ never appears in any connected finite
  cover of $\MPMNS$ detected by subgroup enumeration;
  a degree-12 computation provides a strong test.
\item Neutrino masses (speculative, no direct geometric derivation):
  $\sum m_\nu\approx71$~meV, testable by CMB-S4.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item \emph{Quark CP violation.}
  Explain the Jarlskog scale $J_\mathrm{exp}\approx3\times10^{-5}$
  from a perturbative or symmetry-breaking correction to the
  geometric zeroth-order result $J=0$.
  This is the most pressing phenomenological gap.
\item \emph{Electron mass.}
  Derive $m_e$ from geometric invariants
  (volume $0.9814$, CS$=1/4$, systole).
\item \emph{Hecke eigenvalues.}
  Compute Bianchi modular form Hecke eigenvalues over
  $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ (requires MAGMA); test whether they are
  Lucas numbers, which would provide a proof pathway for
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:lucas}.
\item \emph{Cover prime formula.}
  Prove $H_1(M_n^{(5)})\cong(\ZZ/p_n)^2$,
  $p_n=5n(n+1)+1$, for all $n\geq1$~\cite{Gentry:Farey}.
\item \emph{Word triple selection.}
  Provide a canonical geometric selection principle for the
  word triples, replacing the current scan-based approach.
\item \emph{Volume adjacency.}
  Explain $\mathrm{vol}(\MCKM)\approx\mathrm{vol}(m003_\mathrm{cusp})$
  to $0.05\%$.
\item \emph{Gauge structure.}
  Embed $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$
  in the holonomy framework.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have established three geometric results about two arithmetic
hyperbolic 3-manifolds: a surgery formula proved from the
fundamental group presentation, a trace field dichotomy implying
non-commensurability, and a Lucas prime in the unique algebraic
covering space.
These results are independent of any physical interpretation.

We then presented computational observations suggesting that
the holonomy representations of these manifolds produce
nontrivial numerical correspondences with Standard Model flavor
parameters.
The most precise are the leptonic CP phase ($0.55\%$ error,
no continuous fit parameters) and the $\tau$ lepton mass ratio
($0.006\%$ error, nearest Eisenstein norm).
The most significant open problem is the zeroth-order prediction
$J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$, which requires symmetry-breaking corrections
to match the observed quark CP violation.

The framework is a geometric phenomenology analogous to the
Balmer formula: structured numerical correspondences are
established with precision suggestive of non-random structure;
the dynamical foundation awaits discovery.
