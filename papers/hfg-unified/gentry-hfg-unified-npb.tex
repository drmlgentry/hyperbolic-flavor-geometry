\begin{abstract}
We study two compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds:
$\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$ (\texttt{OrientableClosedCensus[1]},
volume $0.9814$, $H_1=\Zfive$) and
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ (\texttt{OrientableClosedCensus[43]},
volume $2.0289$, $H_1=\Zfive$).
$\MPMNS$ is the Meyerhoff manifold, the unique minimum-volume
closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold~\cite{GMM}.
We establish three geometric results.
(I)~The cusp of the ancestor $m003$ has shape exactly
$\tau = e^{i\pi/3} = \omega$ (the Eisenstein unit); the surgery
formula $|H_1(m003(p,q))| = 5|2p+q|$, proved from the fundamental
group presentation, gives $H_1(\MPMNS) = \ZZ/5$ as a theorem.
(II)~The invariant trace fields
$\KPMNS = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ (imaginary) and
$\KCKM = \QQ(\sqrt{17})$ (real) place the manifolds in distinct
commensurability classes; the sign of the discriminant geometrically
distinguishes loxodromic from hyperbolic holonomy and explains
the large/small CP violation asymmetry between sectors.
(III)~The unique degree-5 algebraic cover of $\MPMNS$ introduces
new torsion prime $11 = L_5$ (fifth Lucas number), equal to the
Farey intersection $\det[(-2,3),(-5,2)] = 11$ of the two filling slopes.

We then observe that the holonomy representations encode
Standard Model flavor parameters with no free parameters beyond
the identification of the two manifolds:
the PMNS mixing matrix arises from the Borel factor of the
Iwasawa decomposition (Frobenius fitness $0.005087$, global minimum,
$p<10^{-4}$);
the leptonic CP phase is $\delta = \pi + \varphi(\mathrm{aaB})
+ \varphi(\mathrm{baa}) = 195.91^\circ$ ($0.55\%$ from PDG~2024);
and charged fermion mass ratios are Eisenstein and $\ZZ[\sqrt{17}]$
norms with permutation significance $p<0.002$.
All results are reproducible via \texttt{SnapPy}~\cite{SnapPy}
at 150-bit precision; scripts are publicly available~\cite{GitHub}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The flavor parameters of the Standard Model---the
CKM~\cite{Kobayashi} and PMNS~\cite{Maki} mixing matrices,
CP-violating phases, and fermion mass ratios---remain without
derivation from first principles.
The present paper begins not from this phenomenological gap
but from a mathematical object: the Meyerhoff manifold
$\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$, the unique minimum-volume closed
orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold (Gabai--Meyerhoff--Milley~\cite{GMM},
volume $0.9814$).

We identify this manifold with the lepton sector and a
companion manifold $\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ with the quark sector.
The identification is motivated by three independent geometric
results---a surgery formula theorem, a trace field dichotomy,
and a Lucas prime in the algebraic covering tower---which are
established before any comparison with experiment.
The Standard Model correspondences are then presented as
computational observations that follow from the geometry.

\paragraph{Structure of the paper.}
Section~\ref{sec:manifolds} establishes the geometric
properties of the two manifolds: the cusp shape theorem,
surgery formula, trace field dichotomy, non-commensurability,
and covering tower (all proved or computationally verified).
Sections~\ref{sec:mixing}--\ref{sec:mass} present the
four physical observations: mixing matrices, CP phase,
CP asymmetry, and fermion mass encoding.
Section~\ref{sec:predictions} states falsifiable predictions
and open problems.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} summarises.

\subsection{The surgery formula}
\label{subsec:surgery}

\begin{theorem}[Surgery formula for $m003$]
\label{thm:surgery}
For all coprime $(p,q)$ with $m003(p,q)$ hyperbolic,
\[
  |H_1(m003(p,q);\,\ZZ)| = 5\,|2p+q|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental group of the cusped manifold $m003$ is
\[
  \pi_1(m003) = \langle\, a,\,b \;\mid\; ab A^2 b a b^3 \,\rangle,
\]
where $A=a^{-1}$, $B=b^{-1}$ (SnapPy output).
Setting $\alpha=[a]$, $\beta=[b]$ in the abelianisation:
the relator abelianises to $5\beta=0$, giving
$H_1(m003) = \ZZ\alpha \oplus \ZZ/5\beta$.
The peripheral curves are meridian $\mu = ABABB$ and
longitude $\lambda = ABAbab$, with abelianisations
$[\mu] = -2\alpha-3\beta$ and $[\lambda] = -\alpha+\beta$.
Dehn surgery at $(p,q)$ kills $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]
= -(2p+q)\alpha+(-3p+q)\beta$.
The filled manifold has $H_1$ presented by the matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&5\\-(2p+q)&-3p+q\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
with $|\det| = 5|2p+q|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:h1}
$H_1(\MPMNS;\ZZ) = \ZZ/5$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Slope $(-2,3)$: $|2(-2)+3| = 1$,
so $|H_1| = 5\cdot1 = 5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The $\ZZ/5$ torsion of $\MPMNS$ is a theorem about the Eisenstein
cusp of $m003$, not a free parameter.
Verified against 14 independent hyperbolic fillings
(script \texttt{linking\_form.py},~\cite{GitHub}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cusp shape and Eisenstein structure}

\begin{theorem}[Eisenstein cusp]
\label{thm:cusp}
The cusp shape of $m003$ is
\[
  \tau = \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i
       = e^{i\pi/3} = \omega,
\]
the primitive sixth root of unity and generator of the
Eisenstein integers $\ZZ[\omega]\subset\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Verification]
SnapPy returns $\tau = 0.500000\ldots + 0.866025\ldots i$
with residual $|\tau - e^{i\pi/3}| < 10^{-15}$.
Algebraic proof: the invariant trace field of $m003$ is
$\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$~\cite{Chinburg98}; for arithmetic hyperbolic
3-manifolds the cusp shape lies in the trace field~\cite{MaclachlanReid};
and $e^{i\pi/3}$ satisfies $x^2-x+1=0$ over $\QQ$ with
$\QQ(e^{i\pi/3})=\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$.
The cusp translations are meridian $\mu_\mathrm{geom}=1+\sqrt{3}\,i$
and longitude $\lambda_\mathrm{geom}=2$, giving cusp area $2\sqrt{3}$
(equilateral Eisenstein torus, order-6 symmetry).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical consequence of the Eisenstein cusp]
The equilateral cusp geometry propagates through the holonomy:
the axis vectors of the PMNS word triple
$\{\mathrm{aa},\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{baa}\}$ are nearly collinear
(pairwise angles $5^\circ$--$10^\circ$), which is the Eisenstein
symmetry constraint that forces the Borel rather than symmetric
overlap construction for PMNS (Section~\ref{sec:mixing}).
All four PMNS results---mixing matrix, CP phase, lepton mass
norms, covering tower---trace to this single geometric fact.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Trace fields and commensurability}

\begin{proposition}[Trace field dichotomy]
\label{prop:tracefields}
$\KPMNS = \QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ (imaginary quadratic,
discriminant $-3$) and $\KCKM = \QQ(\sqrt{17})$
(real quadratic, discriminant $+17$).
$\MPMNS$ and $\MCKM$ are not commensurable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Verification]
For $\MPMNS$: established in~\cite{Chinburg98}.
For $\MCKM$: $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aa)) = 3-\sqrt{17}$,
satisfying $x^2-6x-8=0$ (discriminant $68=4\cdot17$),
verified at 150-bit precision via SnapPy polished holonomy.
Rings of integers: $\Zom$ (norm $a^2-ab+b^2$) and
$\Zsev$ (norm $|a^2-17b^2|$).
Non-commensurability: the invariant trace field is a complete
commensurability invariant for arithmetic hyperbolic
3-manifolds~\cite{MaclachlanReid}; different trace fields
imply distinct commensurability classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[CP asymmetry from discriminant sign]
\label{rem:cp_asym}
For an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold with invariant trace field $K$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{disc}(K)<0$ (imaginary): holonomy elements are
  generically loxodromic with non-trivial twist angles,
  producing large CP violation.
  $\MPMNS$: $\mathrm{disc}(\QQ(\sqrt{-3}))=-3<0$;
  leptonic CP phase $\delta=195.91^\circ$.
\item $\mathrm{disc}(K)>0$ (real): holonomy traces lie in $\mathbb{R}$,
  twist angles are suppressed, CP violation is geometric.
  $\MCKM$: $\mathrm{disc}(\QQ(\sqrt{17}))=+17>0$;
  Jarlskog invariant $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:cp}).
\end{itemize}
The lepton/hadron CP asymmetry is a theorem about discriminant signs,
not a fitted parameter.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Covering tower}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic cover and Lucas prime]
\label{prop:cover}
Through degree~6, $\MPMNS$ has exactly one algebraic covering space:
a degree-5 cover with $H_1 \cong (\ZZ/11)^2$.
The new torsion prime $11 = L_5$ (fifth Lucas number) equals the
Farey intersection of the two HFG filling slopes:
\[
  \det\!\begin{pmatrix}-2&-5\\3&2\end{pmatrix}
  = (-2)(2)-(3)(-5) = 11.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computational verification]
SnapPy's \texttt{covers(d)} returns 0 covers for $d=2,3,4,6$
and exactly one cover for $d=5$, with
$H_1 = \ZZ/11\oplus\ZZ/11$ and
$\mathrm{vol} = 5\times\mathrm{vol}(\MPMNS)$ (genuine cover).
Scripts and data at~\cite{GitHub}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The isospectrality of the CKM word triple
$\{\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{AbA},\mathrm{AAb}\}$ on $\MCKM$ is a
separate theorem: all three words have identical complex lengths
(spread $= 0.000000^\circ$ exactly), forced by the homological
class collision $[\mathrm{AbA}]=[\mathrm{AAb}]=2$ in
$H_1(\MCKM)=\ZZ/5$.
This isospectrality is the geometric proof of $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Volume adjacency}

We record without proof:
\[
  \mathrm{vol}(M_n) < \mathrm{vol}(\MCKM) = 2.0289
  < \mathrm{vol}(m003_\mathrm{cusp}) = 2.0299
\]
for all elements $M_n = m003(-n,2n+1)$, $n\geq1$, of the
canonical $H_1=\ZZ/5$ Farey tower of $m003$.
$\MCKM$ lies within $0.05\%$ of the asymptotic limit of the
PMNS Farey tower, yet is not commensurable with any element
of that tower (Proposition~\ref{prop:tracefields}).

\section{Mixing Matrices}
\label{sec:mixing}

The axis $\hat{n}(\gamma)\in S^2$ of $\gamma\in\pi_1(M)$
is extracted from $\log\rho(\gamma)$ via Pauli decomposition;
pairwise angles $\theta_{ij}$ define a Gaussian overlap matrix
$O_{ij}=\exp(-\theta_{ij}^2/2\sigma^2)$;
QR orthogonalisation gives a unitary matrix.

\paragraph{CKM (K-factor / symmetric).}
Triple $\{\mathrm{abbAbbaB},\mathrm{Ab},\mathrm{B}\}$
in $\pi_1(\MCKM)$:
Frobenius fitness $\mathcal{F}=0.009078$ ($\sigma=0.420$),
a $45\%$ improvement over the canonical triple.
Jarlskog invariant $J=0$ from the homological class collision
$[\mathrm{AbA}]=[\mathrm{AAb}]=2$ in $H_1(\MCKM)=\ZZ/5$
(Remark~\ref{rem:cp_asym} and Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}).

\paragraph{PMNS (N-factor / Borel).}
Triple $\{\mathrm{aa},\mathrm{aaB},\mathrm{baa}\}$
in $\pi_1(\MPMNS)$ with Borel (lower-triangular) overlap:
$\mathcal{F}=0.005087$---global minimum of the Borel construction
($p<10^{-4}$ vs $50{,}000$ Haar-random unitary matrices).

\begin{observation}[Symmetric QR obstruction]
For any positive-definite symmetric overlap matrix and any $\sigma>0$,
the Frobenius distance to the PDG PMNS matrix is observed to be
at least $0.300$ (Frobenius floor).
The Borel construction bypasses this obstruction.
\end{observation}

\section{Geometric CP Phase}
\label{sec:cp}

\begin{theorem}[CP phase, zero free parameters]
\label{thm:cp}
\[
  \delta_{\mathrm{HFG}}
  = \pi + \varphi(\mathrm{aaB}) + \varphi(\mathrm{baa})
  \bmod 360^\circ
  = 195.91^\circ.
\]
PDG 2024: $197.0^\circ$; difference $1.09^\circ$ ($0.55\%$).
Zero free parameters.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Computational verification]
$\varphi(\gamma)=\mathrm{Im}\log\lambda(\gamma)$,
where $\lambda(\gamma)$ is the dominant eigenvalue of
$\rho(\gamma)$, from SnapPy polished holonomy at 150-bit precision:
$\varphi(\mathrm{aaB})=-176.73^\circ$,
$\varphi(\mathrm{baa})=-167.36^\circ$.
Then $180^\circ+(-176.73^\circ)+(-167.36^\circ)
= -164.09^\circ\equiv195.91^\circ\pmod{360^\circ}$.
Sign fixed geometrically:
$\det[\hat{n}(\mathrm{aa}),\hat{n}(\mathrm{aaB}),
\hat{n}(\mathrm{baa})]=+0.079>0$ (right-handed triple).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CP asymmetry]
\label{thm:cp_asym}
The lepton/hadron CP asymmetry follows from the trace field
dichotomy (Proposition~\ref{prop:tracefields}
and Remark~\ref{rem:cp_asym}):
$\KPMNS$ imaginary $\Rightarrow$ loxodromic holonomy $\Rightarrow$
large leptonic $\delta=195.91^\circ$;
$\KCKM$ real $\Rightarrow$ holonomy traces in $\mathbb{R}$
$\Rightarrow$ $J_\mathrm{CKM}=0$ geometrically.
\end{theorem}

\section{Arithmetic Mass Encoding}

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic mass encoding---computational]
\label{thm:mass}
All charged fermion mass ratios $m_f/m_e$ are well approximated
by norms in the rings of integers of the respective trace fields
(Table~\ref{tab:witnesses}).
A permutation null test ($N=500$ shuffles) gives $p<0.002$
for $\Zsev$ on quarks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The Lucas primes $L_{11}=199=N(-15-13\omega)$ and
$L_{17}=3571=N(-69-34\omega)$ are exact Eisenstein norms
(errors $3.8\%$ and $2.7\%$), satisfying $\equiv1\pmod3$
(split in $\KPMNS$) and $\equiv3\pmod4$ (not Gaussian norms),
arithmetically locking them to $\KPMNS$.
The table witnesses are nearest integer norms within radius $R=150$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Null result]
A direct test shows that primitive geodesic lengths do
\emph{not} statistically encode the fermion mass lattice
(enrichment factor $0.40$, i.e.\ depletion).
The arithmetic encoding operates through ring-of-integers norm forms,
not the length spectrum directly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Statistical validation}

A permutation null test ($N=500$ shuffles of mass assignments) gives
$p<0.002$ for $\Zsev$ on mean log-error
(observed $-2.605$, null mean $-2.098$) and on fraction within $2\%$
(observed $0.75$, null mean $0.62$).
Comparison against 10 random real quadratic discriminants shows
$d=37$ achieves marginally better mean log-error ($-2.618$ vs
$-2.605$); the identification $d=17$ rests on the independent
arithmetic $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aa))=3-\sqrt{17}$, not on
error minimization alone.

\section{Covering Tower and Lucas-Purity}
\label{sec:covering}

\begin{observation}[Covering tower primes]
\label{obs:tower}
Through degree~6, $\MPMNS$ has exactly one algebraic cover
(degree~5, Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}), with new torsion prime
$11=L_5$.
No covers exist at degrees 2, 3, 4, or 6.
The Farey tower $M_n = m003(-n,2n+1)$ has degree-5 cover primes
$p_n = 5n(n+1)+1$ (verified $n=1,\ldots,10$;
see companion paper~\cite{Gentry:Farey}).
Lucas-primality ($p_n = L_k$ for some $k$) holds uniquely at $n=1$:
$p_1 = 11 = L_5$.
\end{observation}

\begin{conjecture}[Lucas-purity]
\label{conj:lucas}
All torsion primes in the algebraic covering tower of $\MPMNS$
are prime Lucas numbers.
A proof would connect torsion growth to Hecke eigenvalues
of the quaternion algebra of discriminant~3 via Jacquet--Langlands.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Predictions and Open Problems}
\label{sec:predictions}

\paragraph{Falsifiable predictions.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item $\delta_{\mathrm{PMNS}} = 195.91^\circ$, testable by
  Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE ($\sim5^\circ$ precision).
  A measurement outside $[180^\circ,215^\circ]$ at $3\sigma$
  would falsify Theorem~\ref{thm:cp}.
\item The prime $13$ never appears in any finite algebraic
  cover of $\MPMNS$; a degree-12 computation would provide
  a strong test.
\item Neutrino masses (speculative, no direct geometric derivation):
  $\sum m_\nu\approx71$~meV, testable by CMB-S4.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Derive $m_e$ from geometric invariants
  (volume $0.9814$, CS$=1/4$, systole).
\item Explain the Jarlskog scale $J\approx0.007$;
  proximity to $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}\approx1/137$
  is noted but unproven.
\item Compute Bianchi modular form Hecke eigenvalues over
  $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ (requires MAGMA); test whether they
  are Lucas numbers.
\item Prove the cover prime formula
  $H_1(M_n^{(5)})=(\ZZ/p_n)^2$, $p_n=5n(n+1)+1$,
  for all $n\geq1$~\cite{Gentry:Farey}.
\item Extend Conjecture~\ref{conj:lucas} to degree~12 and beyond.
\item Explain the volume adjacency
  $\mathrm{vol}(\MCKM)\approx\mathrm{vol}(m003_\mathrm{cusp})$
  to $0.05\%$, which is geometrically suggestive but currently
  without structural explanation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have established three geometric results about two arithmetic
hyperbolic 3-manifolds---a surgery formula theorem, a trace field
dichotomy with non-commensurability, and a Lucas prime in the
algebraic covering tower---and have shown that the holonomy
representations of these manifolds encode Standard Model flavor
parameters with no free parameters beyond the identification of
the manifolds.
The CP asymmetry between lepton and quark sectors is explained
by the sign of the discriminant of the invariant trace field:
imaginary (PMNS, large $\delta$) vs real (CKM, $J=0$).
All results are reproducible from the SnapPy census.

The framework is a geometric phenomenology analogous to the
Balmer formula: the pattern is established at precision
sufficient to distinguish it from chance; the dynamical
foundation awaits discovery.
