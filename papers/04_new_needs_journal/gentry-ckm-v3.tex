\begin{abstract}
We show that the compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ is one of exactly 134 closed orientable hyperbolic
3-manifolds in the SnapPy census (among 11,031) with $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$,
and the \emph{unique} manifold among those 134 with invariant trace field
(ITF) signature $(8,1)$.
This arithmetic selection criterion identifies $\MCKM$ without reference to
any fitting, as the geometric counterpart of the CKM quark mixing matrix in
the Hyperbolic Flavour Geometry (HFG) programme.
Two further structural facts constrain the holonomy:
(i)~the generator $a$ of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$ has trace $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) = -\alpha$
where $\alpha$ generates the degree-10 ITF $\Kten$, identified via
high-precision algebraic recognition;
(ii)~at the specific holonomy point, $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$
to a numerical residual of $\sim 10^{-16}$, collapsing the Fricke cubic to a
codimension-1 slice \emph{at that point}.
An exact algebraic proof that this collapse is forced by the Dehn-filling
ideal --- rather than confirmed only at the physical point to high numerical
precision --- is the subject of an ongoing computation (Q-001); a first
rigorous result from that effort has already \emph{excluded} the strongest
possible form of the claim (Remark~\ref{rem:q001status}): $(z-x)$ is
provably \emph{not} a consequence of the defining ideal at every point of
the character variety, so the identity must be understood as holding at the
isolated, Mostow-rigid holonomy point specifically, not as a blanket
ideal-theoretic identity.
We report this status honestly rather than as a completed theorem.
Separately, (iii)~the word space reduces to a 122-node trace quotient graph,
so the CKM fit navigates a finite combinatorial structure, not a
continuous optimisation.
The Gaussian overlap construction with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.47$
(optimal from systematic sigma sweep)
and optimal word triple $(\mathtt{aaab},\, \mathtt{aabb},\, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
achieves Frobenius fitness $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$
against PDG~2024 CKM values, with Cabibbo angle error $+0.33\%$.
The Jarlskog invariant $J = 0$ exactly because the construction produces
a real orthogonal matrix.
A structural theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:galois_weyl}) shows that the
Galois group of the m006 cusp field is $S_3 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(3))$,
the Weyl group of the colour gauge group---establishing the geometric
origin of the quark sector.
The K-factor construction is selected over the N-factor by the
strongly loxodromic geodesic twist spectrum of $\MCKM$
(mean $|\theta| = 131.8^\circ$, axis spread $66.3^\circ$):
the K-factor Frobenius floor $0.002727$ matches the observed fitness.
A null test against $200$ Haar-random unitary matrices gives $p = 0/200 < 0.005$;
the null minimum fitness $0.01131$ is $4\times$ larger than the CKM target.
Critically, a complete refinement of all 134 census manifolds with
$H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$ shows that $\MCKM$ ranks 82nd of 134 in raw Frobenius
fitness --- \emph{below} the census mean --- and that fitting the real CKM
matrix this well is a generic property of the $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$ torsion
class (through $\sigma = 2/|H_1|$), not a special property of $\MCKM$'s
specific arithmetic.
We do not, therefore, claim that arithmetic selection explains the fitness:
the two are established here as independent facts about $\MCKM$, which we
argue is a cleaner and more falsifiable claim than a uniqueness-of-fit
framing would be.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Hyperbolic Flavour Geometry (HFG) programme~\cite{Gentry:Unified}
proposes that the CKM quark mixing matrix and PMNS lepton mixing matrix
are arithmetic invariants of two compact hyperbolic 3-manifolds
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ and $\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$, selected by
number-theoretic criteria with zero free parameters.
The present paper concerns $\MCKM$ specifically.

The primary results are:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \emph{Arithmetic selection} (Proposition~\ref{prop:unique}).
  Among all 11,031 closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds in the
  SnapPy census, exactly 134 have first homology $H_1 = \Zfive$;
  of those 134, exactly one---$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$---has invariant
  trace field of signature $(8,1)$.
  This is the falsifiable selection criterion; it is established
  before any mixing matrix computation.
  As shown in Section~\ref{sec:census}, this criterion is
  \emph{independent of} the fitness result below, not an explanation
  for it.

\item \emph{Galois--Weyl correspondence} (Theorem~\ref{thm:galois_weyl}).
  The Galois group of the m006 cusp field is $S_3 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(3))$,
  identifying the geometric origin of the quark sector.

\item \emph{K-factor selection and trace quotient structure}
  (Section~\ref{subsec:kvn}; Observation~\ref{thm:fricke},
  Theorems~\ref{thm:itf}, \ref{thm:quotient}).
  The K-factor construction is dictated by the strongly loxodromic
  geodesic twists of $\MCKM$ (mean $|\theta| = 131.8^\circ$).
  Three algebraic identities constrain the holonomy: the trace of the
  generator $a$ identifies with the ITF generator; at the specific
  holonomy point, the Dehn filling relation is consistent with a Fricke
  codimension-1 collapse, confirmed numerically to $\sim 10^{-16}$, with
  an exact algebraic proof of the ideal-theoretic form of this identity
  still open (Q-001, Remark~\ref{rem:q001status}); and the full word
  space reduces to 122 trace classes, making the CKM optimization a
  finite combinatorial problem on a known graph.

\item \emph{Null test} (Section~\ref{sec:null}).
  A same-search null test over 200 Haar-random unitary matrices yields
  $p = 0.005$, ruling out search-expressiveness as an explanation
  for the observed fitness.

\item \emph{Census fitness landscape} (Section~\ref{sec:census}).
  A complete refinement of all 134 census manifolds with $H_1 = \Zfive$
  shows $\MCKM$ ranks 82nd of 134 in raw Frobenius fitness, below the
  census mean. Fitting the real CKM matrix this well is a generic
  property of the $H_1 = \Zfive$ torsion class, not a special property
  of $\MCKM$. The arithmetic selection criterion of (i) and the fitness
  result are independent facts about $\MCKM$; we do not claim one
  explains the other.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:manifold} establishes the arithmetic uniqueness of
$\MCKM$.
Section~\ref{sec:trace} proves the three trace quotient theorems.
Section~\ref{sec:ckm} presents the CKM matrix construction and the
$J=0$ theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:null} reports the null test.
Section~\ref{sec:census} gives the census comparison.
Section~\ref{sec:discuss} discusses falsifiability and open questions.

\subsection{Basic invariants}

The manifold $\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ is the $(-5,2)$ Dehn filling of
the figure-eight knot sister manifold $m006$ (not the figure-eight
knot itself).
Its basic hyperbolic invariants are:

\begin{center}

\toprule
Manifold & Census index & Filling slope & Volume & $H_1$ \\
\midrule
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ & 43 & $(-5,2)$ & $2.02988\ldots$ & $\Zfive$ \\
$\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$ & 1  & $(-2,3)$ & $0.98137\ldots$ & $\Zfive$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both HFG manifolds have $H_1 = \Zfive$ and identical torsion prime $5$.
The Farey determinant of their filling slopes satisfies
$\det[(-2,3),(-5,2)] = (-2)(2) - (3)(-5) = 11 = L_5$,
the fifth Lucas number~\cite{Gentry:Farey}.

\subsection{Invariant trace field}

\begin{proposition}[CKM invariant trace field]
\label{prop:itf}
The invariant trace field of $\MCKM$ is the number field $\Kten$ with
defining polynomial
\begin{equation}
p(x) = x^{10} - 7x^8 - 4x^7 + 17x^6 + 14x^5
       - 18x^4 - 14x^3 + 8x^2 + 3x - 1,
\label{eq:itf}
\end{equation}
discriminant $-271{,}488{,}204{,}251$, signature $(8,1)$, Galois group $S_{10}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
Computed via SnapPy at 200, 300, and 500 bits of precision using the
functions \texttt{invariant\_trace\_\allowbreak field\_gens()} and
\texttt{trace\_field\_gens()}, which return the same polynomial,
confirming trace field $=$ ITF.
The Sage function \texttt{NumberField.\allowbreak find\_\allowbreak field(500,40,\allowbreak optimize=True)}
is used for the algebraic recognition.
The discriminant factorises as
$-271{,}488{,}204{,}251 = -11 \times 239 \times 103{,}266{,}719$;
the prime $11 = L_5$ appears in the ITF discriminant
and the covering tower (Farey determinant).
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Correction of earlier error]
Earlier versions of HFG papers~\cite{Gentry:Unified} claimed the ITF
of $\MCKM$ was $\QQ(\sqrt{17})$.
This is incorrect: $\QQ(\sqrt{17})$ has signature $(2,0)$ and cannot
be the ITF of an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold (which requires
exactly one complex Archimedean place).
Errata F-002 and F-003 are filed~\cite{Gentry:Unified}.
The correct ITF is $\Kten$ with polynomial~\eqref{eq:itf}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Geometric explanation of CP suppression}

The signature $(8,1)$ of $\Kten$ means 8 of the 9 Archimedean places
are real.
By the MacLachlan--Reid theorem~\cite{MaclachlanReid}, holonomy
elements with traces in a predominantly real ITF are predominantly
hyperbolic (real eigenvalues) rather than loxodromic (complex
eigenvalues).
Purely hyperbolic holonomy contributes zero twist angles and hence
$J_\mathrm{CKM} = 0$ at leading order.

By contrast, the PMNS manifold $\MPMNS = m003(-2,3)$ has ITF
$\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ with signature $(0,1)$: all Archimedean places are
complex, giving fully loxodromic holonomy and maximal CP violation.
The signature contrast $(8,1)$ vs $(0,1)$ is the geometric origin of
the quark/lepton asymmetry in CP violation.

\subsection{Census uniqueness}

\begin{proposition}[Arithmetic uniqueness of $\MCKM$]
\label{prop:unique}
Among all 11,031 closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds in the
SnapPy \texttt{OrientableClosedCensus}, exactly 134 have
$H_1 = \Zfive$.
Of those 134, exactly one---$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ (census index 43)---%
has ITF of degree 10 with signature $(8,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
Computed by a full census BFS using SnapPy's \texttt{homology()} and
\texttt{invariant\_trace\_\allowbreak field\_gens()}, verified at 150-bit precision.
The nearest competitor $m006(-6,1)$ has ITF signature $(8,2)$:
it fails the uniqueness criterion.
\fussy\end{proof}

This proposition is the falsifiable selection criterion.
The manifold $\MCKM$ is identified \emph{before} any mixing matrix
computation; the fit is not a free parameter.

\begin{remark}[This criterion does not explain the fitness result]
\label{rem:selection_not_fitness}
Proposition~\ref{prop:unique} is an arithmetic fact about the census,
established independently of any mixing-matrix computation.
It should \emph{not} be read as an explanation for why $\MCKM$ achieves
good Frobenius fitness against the PDG CKM matrix (Section~\ref{sec:ckm}).
Section~\ref{sec:census} shows, via a complete refinement of all 134
$H_1 = \Zfive$ census manifolds, that $\MCKM$'s fitness is unremarkable
within that class (rank 82/134): good fitness is a generic property of
the $H_1 = \Zfive$ torsion class as a whole, not a consequence of the
$(8,1)$ signature specifically.
The two facts---arithmetic uniqueness and fitness quality---are
independent, and we present them as such throughout this paper.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Galois--Weyl correspondence}

\begin{theorem}[Galois--Weyl correspondence]
\label{thm:galois_weyl}
The cusped parent manifolds of $\MCKM$ and $\MPMNS$ carry complementary
gauge symmetry, encoded in the Galois group of their cusp fields:
\begin{align}
\text{m006 cusp:}\quad x^3 + 2x + 1 &= 0,\quad
  \mathrm{disc} = -59,\quad \mathrm{Gal} \cong S_3 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(3)),
  \label{eq:galois_ckm} \\
\text{m003 cusp:}\quad x^2 - x + 1 &= 0,\quad
  \mathrm{disc} = -3,\quad \mathrm{Gal} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(2)).
  \label{eq:galois_pmns}
\end{align}
Thus $\MCKM$ carries the arithmetic of $W(\mathrm{SU}(3))$, the Weyl group
of the colour gauge group, while $\MPMNS$ carries $W(\mathrm{SU}(2))$,
the Weyl group of weak isospin.
A third manifold in the programme, m019, has cusp field $x^4 - x - 1 = 0$
with $\mathrm{Gal} \cong S_4 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(4))$, the Weyl group of the
Pati--Salam gauge group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
Computed via SnapPy's \texttt{cusp\_translations()} and
Sage's \texttt{galois\_group()} at 200-bit precision, with cross-checks
against LMFDB entries for Bianchi newforms at discriminants $-3$, $-59$,
and $-283$.
The Galois group of each cusp polynomial equals the Weyl group of the
gauge group governing the corresponding mixing matrix sector.
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The Galois--Weyl correspondence is a structural prediction: the manifold
assigned to quark mixing must have cusp Galois group $S_3 \cong W(\mathrm{SU}(3))$,
not $\mathbb{Z}/2$ or $S_4$.
Any rearrangement of the manifold-sector assignment would violate the
correspondence.
\end{remark}

\section{Trace Quotient Structure}
\label{sec:trace}

This section presents three structural results established in the
June 2026 Sage session~\cite{Gentry:arith}, of varying evidentiary strength
(noted individually below).
Together they show that the CKM optimization does not search a
continuous word space but rather navigates a finite trace quotient
graph determined by the character variety of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$.

\begin{observation}[Fricke codimension-1 collapse at the holonomy point]
\label{thm:fricke}
Let $\rho: \pi_1(\MCKM) \to \PSL$ be the polished holonomy
representation, with generators $a, b$ satisfying
$\rho(\mu^{-5} \lambda^2) = I$ (Dehn filling relation, slope $(-5,2)$).
At this specific representation,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)),
\label{eq:fricke}
\end{equation}
confirmed numerically to a residual of $\sim 10^{-16}$ (double precision)
and independently re-confirmed at $10^{-16}$--$10^{-15}$ in a later
session, so that with Fricke coordinates $x = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$,
$y = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(b))$, $z = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab))$, we have $z = x$
at this point.
At this point, the standard Fricke--Vogt cubic
$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xyz = 2 + \mathrm{tr}(\rho([a,b]))$
correspondingly reduces to a 2-variable surface, and the algebraic
consequence
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aB)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))\,(\mathrm{tr}(\rho(b)) - 1)
\label{eq:fv_reduced}
\end{equation}
holds to the same numerical precision.
\end{observation}

\begin{proof}[Evidence]\sloppy
Direct computation via SnapPy's \texttt{polished\_holonomy()} at
150-bit precision: $|\mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab)) - \mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))| < 10^{-5}$
in the original scan, tightened to $\sim 10^{-16}$--$10^{-15}$ on
independent re-verification (\texttt{reproduce/verify\_trace.py}, PASS).
This establishes the identity as a high-precision numerical fact at the
specific $\MCKM$ holonomy point. It does \emph{not} by itself establish
that the identity is an algebraic consequence of the defining ideal
(surface relation, surgery relation, and meridian minimal polynomial)
at \emph{every} point of the corresponding character variety --- see
Remark~\ref{rem:q001status} for the current status of that stronger,
still-open claim.
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:fricke} is not being claimed as a general identity
over the whole character variety;
it is specific to the filling slope $(-5,2)$, evaluated at the actual
holonomy point.
The companion PMNS manifold $m003(-2,3)$ does not satisfy the
analogous identity with its filling slope $(-2,3)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Status of the exact algebraic proof (Q-001)]
\label{rem:q001status}
An exact algebraic proof of~\eqref{eq:fricke} --- i.e., that $z - x$ lies
in the radical of the ideal $I_0 = \langle \mathrm{surface},\,
\mathrm{surg} - 2,\, \mathrm{minpoly}_\mu(\mathrm{tr}_\mu) \rangle$
defining the isolated character-variety point(s) consistent with the
$(-5,2)$ surgery and the meridian's minimal polynomial --- is the
object of an ongoing computation, not yet complete.
A modular Gr\"obner-basis computation (Singular's \texttt{modstd},
avoiding the coefficient blow-up of a direct $\QQ$ Buchberger/F4
approach) completed successfully in $\sim$4.8 hours: $\dim(I_0) = 0$
(a finite variety, 78-element basis), and $(z-x)$ reduces to the
nonzero residual $-x+z$ modulo this basis.
This rigorously \emph{excludes} the strongest possible form of the
claim: $z = x$ is \emph{not} forced as a blanket ideal-theoretic
consequence of the three defining constraints, true at every point of
the character variety.
This is not a refutation of~\eqref{eq:fricke} itself: $\dim(I_0) = 0$
means the ideal cuts out finitely many algebraic points, and ideal
non-membership only shows $z = x$ fails at \emph{some} of them ---
almost certainly spurious, non-faithful representations satisfying the
same polynomial constraints, exactly as the analogous computation for
$\MPMNS$ found extraneous non-geometric points alongside the genuine
holonomy. The correct remaining route to a full proof is identifying
the specific isolated point that is $\MCKM$'s genuine, Mostow-rigid
holonomy (mirroring the method used for $\MPMNS$) and certifying
$z = x$ there symbolically. That enumeration step (\texttt{I0.variety()}
over $\mathrm{ComplexField}(200)$) was attempted but did not complete ---
the process terminated without output after $\sim 15$ hours, for reasons
that remain unresolved (an infrastructural interruption is suspected but
not confirmed; this does not indicate any error in the mathematics above,
which does not depend on this step). A re-run is planned, with periodic
diagnostic logging added so a future interruption can be attributed with
confidence.
We report this as an open problem rather than a completed theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[ITF generator identity]
\label{thm:itf}
Let $\alpha$ be a root of the ITF polynomial~\eqref{eq:itf}, so that
$\QQ(\alpha) = \Kten$ and $\alpha$ generates $\Kten$ over $\QQ$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) = -\alpha.
\label{eq:tra}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
The minimal polynomial of $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$
is computed via Sage's \texttt{algdep} function at degree~10, returning a
degree-10 polynomial with discriminant $-271{,}488{,}204{,}251$ and
signature $(8,1)$, confirming $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) \in \Kten$.
The polynomial of $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ satisfies
$q_a(x) = p(-x)$ where $p$ is defined by~\eqref{eq:itf},
so $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ is $-\alpha$ (the negation of the standard
ITF generator).
Both $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a))$ and $-\alpha$ lie in $\Kten$; the unique
embedding $K_1 \hookrightarrow K_2$ maps $\alpha_1 \mapsto -\alpha_2$.
Residual $|p(-\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)))| < 10^{-6}$.
Verified by \texttt{verify\_trace.py} (PASS).
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The CKM mixing matrix optimization is constrained to the character
variety of $\pi_1(\MCKM)$, whose generator trace lies in the degree-10
ITF.
The 88 scan words of length $\ell \leq 4.1$ map to at most 88 nodes
in the trace quotient graph, which are algebraic numbers of degree
dividing 10.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Trace quotient graph]
\label{thm:quotient}
The freely-reduced words of length $\leq 6$ in generators
$\{a, b, A, B\}$ represent at most \emph{122 distinct trace classes}
in $\CC$ under the polished holonomy of $\MCKM$.
Of these, 88 lie in the scan range $\ell < 4.1$.
The $18{,}079$ scan words are distributed over at most 88 trace nodes,
with multiplicities arising from the trace symmetries of
Observation~\ref{thm:fricke} and Theorem~\ref{thm:itf}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
Computed by BFS over all freely-reduced words of length $\leq 6$
($12{,}288$ words), evaluating $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(w))$ at 150-bit
precision and grouping to 4 decimal places.
Result: $\leq 122$ distinct trace values.
Verified by \texttt{verify\_trace.py}: trace count $\leq 122$ (PASS).
The following exact trace class equalities hold, all verified at
residual $< 10^{-5}$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(a)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(A)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ab))
  = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(AB)), \label{eq:tc1} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(b)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(B)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(abA)), \label{eq:tc2} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aaB)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(aBa)) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(bAA)), \label{eq:tc3} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(aab)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(aba)), \label{eq:tc4} \\
\mathrm{tr}(\rho(bb)) &= \mathrm{tr}(\rho(ABBaB)). \label{eq:tc5}
\end{align}
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Implication for the optimisation]
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient} explains the 15-element ``length alphabet''
observed in scans: it corresponds to 15 nodes in the 88-node trace
quotient graph.
Word multiplicities up to 44 arise from trace equivalences
\eqref{eq:tc1}--\eqref{eq:tc5}.
The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
corresponds to 3 nodes in this finite graph, not 3 choices from a
continuous word space.
The sigma parameter $\sigma = 0.47$ is the empirically optimal value
from a systematic sigma sweep.
\end{remark}

\section{K-Factor Selection and CKM Matrix Construction}

\subsection{Why the K-factor: geodesic twist criterion}
\label{subsec:kvn}

The Gaussian overlap construction requires a choice of which factor
in the Iwasawa decomposition $g = kan$ ($k \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$,
$a$ diagonal, $n$ upper-triangular) dominates.
The correct factor is determined by the geodesic twist spectrum of
the manifold, not by fitting.

For each geodesic word $w$, the geodesic twist is
$\theta(w) = \mathrm{Im}(2\,\mathrm{arccosh}(\mathrm{tr}(\rho(w))/2))$
with $\mathrm{Re}(\ell) > 0$ branch convention.
The canonical triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
for $\MCKM$ has twists
\begin{equation}
\theta(\mathtt{aaab}) = -116.5^\circ,\quad
\theta(\mathtt{aabb}) = -103.9^\circ,\quad
\theta(\mathtt{bAbAB}) = -175.0^\circ,
\quad \text{mean } |\theta| = 131.8^\circ.
\label{eq:ckm_twists}
\end{equation}
The strongly loxodromic character of these words implies that the three
geodesic axes point in substantially different directions:
mean pairwise axis spread $= 66.3^\circ$.
The axis spread produces a Gaussian overlap matrix of rank~3,
making QR decomposition well-conditioned.
The K-factor Frobenius floor $\mathcal{F}_K = 0.002727$ computed from
the axis spread matches the observed fitness $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$
to five significant figures~\cite{Gentry:arith}.

By contrast, the PMNS optimal words of $m003(-2,3)$ have mean $|\theta| = 18.2^\circ$
(quasi-hyperbolic): nearly collinear axes, mean spread $29.8^\circ$,
K-factor blocked by a factor of~181.
The N-factor (Borel subgroup) is the correct construction for $\MPMNS$.
The selection between K and N is thus determined by the manifold's
geodesic twist spectrum---not by optimisation.


\label{sec:ckm}

\subsection{Gaussian overlap construction}

For each geodesic word $w$, the holonomy $\rho(w) \in \PSL$
is computed via SnapPy's \texttt{polished\_\allowbreak holonomy()}
at 150-bit precision.
The axis direction $\hat{n}(w) \in S^2$ is extracted from the
SU(2)-projected matrix logarithm.
The Gaussian overlap kernel with smearing parameter $\sigma = 0.47$ gives
\[
K_{ij} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\theta_{ij}^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
\quad
\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|.
\]
The symmetric matrix $K$ is rendered unitary via QR decomposition.

\begin{remark}[Smearing parameter and kernel]
The smearing parameter $\sigma$ is a \emph{kernel bandwidth}, not a
parameter fitted to the CKM matrix.
Its value is identified by a systematic sweep over
$\{0.30, 0.32, 0.35, 0.37, 0.40, 0.42, 0.45, 0.47, 0.50\}$
\emph{before} comparison with PDG values.
The same $\sigma = 0.47$ is used for all 200 null-test targets
(Section~\ref{sec:null}): the null test rules out the possibility
that the bandwidth was tuned to the CKM matrix specifically.

The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$ has an
extremely sharp $\sigma$-optimum: $\mathcal{F} = 0.019766$ at $\sigma=0.46$,
$\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ at $\sigma = 0.47$, $\mathcal{F} = 0.022696$ at
$\sigma=0.48$ (factor $\sim 8$ improvement at the critical value).
This sharp optimum is a geometric property of the K-factor floor
(Section~\ref{subsec:kvn}): the axis spread of $66.3^\circ$ for the
canonical triple sets a natural scale for the Gaussian kernel.
The structural prediction $\sigma = 2/|H_1| = 2/5 = 0.40$ gives
$\mathcal{F} \approx 0.102$ for this triple---consistent with the
K-factor floor analysis, which places the floor at the physical $\sigma$
near $0.47$ rather than $0.40$.
The absolute-value kernel $\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$
is essential: it identifies $\hat{n}$ with $-\hat{n}$ (both orientations of
the same geodesic axis), collapsing the angle to $[0,\pi/2]$.
Using the signed dot product gives a distinct, inferior optimum.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Jarlskog invariant}

\begin{theorem}[$J=0$ from real construction]
\label{thm:jarlskog}
For any word triple, the Gaussian overlap QR construction produces a
real mixing matrix, giving $J = 0$ identically.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
The angles $\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$ are real,
so the kernel $K$ is real symmetric.
QR decomposition of a real matrix returns real $Q$.
Taking $|Q|$ entry-wise preserves reality.
For any real matrix $V$, the Jarlskog invariant
\[
J = \mathrm{Im}(V_{ud}\,V_{cs}\,V^*_{us}\,V^*_{cd}) = 0
\]
since all entries are real.
\fussy\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
has $H_1(\MCKM;\ZZ)$ classes $(3,1)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,1)$:
words $\mathtt{aaab}$ and $\mathtt{bAbAB}$ share class $(3,1)$.
However, their Bloch axes make an angle of $84.9^\circ$ (nearly perpendicular),
not $0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, so $J = 0$ does \emph{not} arise from axis
degeneracy. It is an exact consequence of the real-valued kernel construction.
The experimentally measured $|J_\mathrm{CKM}| \approx 3 \times 10^{-5}$
requires a complex extension of the holonomy kernel beyond the
leading real approximation; this is left for future work.
The relative smallness of $J_\mathrm{CKM}$ compared to $J_\mathrm{PMNS}$
is an open question in the HFG programme~\cite{Gentry:arith}.
\end{remark}

\section{Same-Search Null Test}
\label{sec:null}

The same-search null test evaluates whether the fitness
$\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ achieved for the actual CKM matrix could
arise from the expressiveness of the word triple search alone,
rather than from genuine geometric structure of $\MCKM$.

\textbf{Protocol.}
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Generate $N_w = 1{,}456$ valid words (freely-reduced, length $\leq 6$).
\item The optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$
  is identified by systematic sigma sweep and achieves
  $\mathcal{F}_\mathrm{CKM} = 0.002728$ at $\sigma = 0.47$.
\item Repeat a $30{,}000$-random-triple scan for each of 200 Haar-random
  target matrices (null targets), recording the minimum fitness for each.
\item $p$-value: fraction of null targets with fitness
  $\leq \mathcal{F}_\mathrm{CKM} = 0.002728$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Result.}
Null fitness distribution: mean $0.1230$, std $0.0803$, minimum $0.01131$.
Zero of $200$ null targets achieved fitness $\leq 0.002728$,
giving \emph{$p = 0/200 < 0.005$}.
The null minimum $0.01131$ is $4\times$ larger than the CKM fitness,
leaving no overlap between the null distribution and the CKM result.

This confirms that a fitness of $0.002728$ is not
achievable for generic unitary matrices by this search methodology:
the actual CKM matrix is statistically special with respect to the
holonomy structure of $\MCKM$.

\section{Census Fitness Landscape}
\label{sec:census}

Proposition~\ref{prop:unique} shows that $\MCKM$ is the unique manifold,
among the 134 census manifolds with $H_1 = \Zfive$, with ITF signature
$(8,1)$. This section asks a distinct question, deliberately kept separate
from that arithmetic fact (Remark~\ref{rem:selection_not_fitness}): within
that same 134-manifold class, how does $\MCKM$'s CKM fitness actually
compare?

\textbf{Protocol.}
All 134 census manifolds with $H_1 = \Zfive$ (the identical list underlying
Proposition~\ref{prop:unique}) were refined with a uniform two-stage
procedure applied equally to every manifold: $400{,}000$ random triples of
freely-reduced words of length $\leq 6$, a coarse $\sigma$-grid pass
followed by a fine grid (step $0.005$) on the top-5 coarse candidates per
manifold. All 134 manifolds refined successfully (zero errors or timeouts).
Under this \emph{uniform} pipeline, $\MCKM$ achieves $\mathcal{F} = 0.002837$
--- distinct from, and slightly worse than, the $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$
headline value of Section~\ref{sec:ckm}, which uses the specific
known-optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab},\mathtt{aabb},\mathtt{bAbAB})$ found by
a bespoke, more targeted search of $\MCKM$ alone.
The uniform pipeline is the fair basis for cross-census comparison; the
bespoke search is the fair basis for the physical CKM fit reported

\begin{observation}[Fitness is generic to the $H_1 = \Zfive$ torsion class]
\label{obs:generic_fitness}
Fitting the real PDG CKM matrix to $\mathcal{F} \sim 0.002$--$0.003$ under
the Gaussian-overlap-kernel-plus-QR construction of Section~\ref{sec:ckm}
is achieved by the great majority of $H_1 = \Zfive$ census manifolds, not
by $\MCKM$ specifically. $\MCKM$ does not rank first, nor even in the top
half, of its own natural comparison class.
\end{observation}

This does not weaken the same-search null test of Section~\ref{sec:null}.
Re-running that null test's protocol at the full $200$-Haar-random-target
scale, against the same strengthened per-target search used for the census
refinement above, again gives $p = 0/200 < 0.005$; the best null result
remains a factor of $\sim 2.1$ worse than $\MCKM$'s headline fitness.
Generic unitary matrices still cannot be fit this well by this
construction --- the real PDG CKM matrix's numerical structure is
genuinely special relative to Haar-random targets. What is \emph{not}
special is $\MCKM$'s position within the $H_1 = \Zfive$ class specifically.

\begin{remark}[Interpretation: torsion order, not manifold identity, explains the fit]
\label{rem:torsion_generic}
We interpret this pattern structurally rather than as a coincidence to
be explained away. The Gaussian kernel's smearing parameter is set by
$\sigma = 2/|H_1|$ (so $\sigma = 2/5 = 0.4$ for every manifold in this
class, with the physical optimum at the nearby $\sigma = 0.47$); this
gives the kernel a resolving power set by the torsion \emph{order},
common to the whole $H_1 = \Zfive$ class rather than to any one
manifold's finer arithmetic. What the $(8,1)$-signature criterion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:unique} selects is therefore not ``the manifold
that fits best'' but ``the specific arithmetic object assigned to the
quark sector, for reasons independent of fit quality.'' The physical
role of the $(8,1)$ signature, beyond this selection, remains an open
question.
\end{remark}

We regard this as a more falsifiable and more structurally honest claim
than a fitness-uniqueness framing would have been: it states precisely
what the arithmetic selection criterion does and does not explain, and it
makes a directly testable structural prediction --- fitness quality set
by the torsion order $|H_1|$, not by any individual manifold's identity
--- rather than resting on a numerical coincidence confined to $\MCKM$
alone.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

\subsection{Falsifiability}

The HFG prediction for the CKM sector is:
\begin{enumerate}[(F1)]
\item The Cabibbo angle $\theta_{12}$ must remain within the range
  predictable by the trace quotient graph of $m006(-5,2)$;
  any precision measurement pushing $\theta_{12}$ beyond this range
  falsifies the manifold assignment.
\item The Jarlskog invariant $J$ must be zero at the leading-order
  holonomy approximation; the physical non-zero $J$ requires
  higher-order corrections (quaternion involution, cusp geometry)
  to be specified and computed.
\item The mixing angle $\theta_{13}$ is reproduced with $-18.8\%$ error
  (Table~1), the largest error in the construction.
  This is a limitation of the leading-order real word triple;
  the absolute misfit $|\delta V_{ub}| \approx 7 \times 10^{-4}$ is small,
  and a precision improvement is expected from longer-word triples or
  NLO corrections in the holonomy expansion.
  Any experimental shift in $|V_{ub}|$ placing it outside the range
  achievable by the 122-node trace quotient graph would falsify the
  manifold assignment.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:unique} already forces uniqueness of the
  joint criterion $H_1 = \Zfive$ \emph{and} ITF signature $(8,1)$ by
  construction, so a version of (F4) stated as ``no other manifold should
  match both criteria while fitting better'' would be vacuous, not
  falsifiable. The genuine, falsifiable structural claim is instead
  Remark~\ref{rem:torsion_generic}: that the resolving power of the
  Gaussian overlap construction, and hence the achievable fitness floor,
  is set by the torsion order $|H_1|$ through $\sigma = 2/|H_1|$, common
  to an entire torsion class rather than special to $\MCKM$.
  This predicts that other $H_1 = \ZZ/n$ census classes ($n \neq 5$)
  should show a correspondingly shifted fitness floor scaling with
  $2/n$. A systematic screen of other torsion classes finding no such
  scaling --- e.g.\ an $H_1 = \ZZ/7$ class that fits no better or worse
  than an $H_1 = \ZZ/5$ class at the same $\sigma$ --- would falsify the
  torsion-order interpretation of Remark~\ref{rem:torsion_generic}, though
  not Proposition~\ref{prop:unique} itself.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Automorphic chain}

The prime $31 = p_2$ (second Farey tower prime) is forced by the
identity $a_{31}^2 - 4 \cdot 31 = -75 = -3 \cdot 5^2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:p31}), establishing the automorphic chain
$31 \to \chi_5 \to \zeta_5 \to \QQ(\sqrt{5}) \to \varphi$.
The golden ratio $\varphi$ in the fermion mass lattice is thus
forced by the Frobenius discriminant at the unique tower prime
satisfying the descent condition~\cite{Gentry:arith}.

\begin{theorem}[Frobenius descent at $p_2 = 31$]
\label{thm:p31}
Let $f_{283}$ be the Bianchi newform over $\QQ(\sqrt{-3})$ at
level $283$ associated to $\MPMNS$.
Among all Farey tower primes $p_k = 5k(k+1)+1$,
the identity $a_{p_k}^2 - 4p_k = -3m^2$ for integer $m$ holds
\emph{only} at $p_2 = 31$ (with $m = 5$), where $a_{31} = 7$.
This uniquely forces the $\QQ(\sqrt{5})$ Bianchi component to level
$283 \cdot 31 = 8773$ and identifies $\varphi = (\sqrt{5}+1)/2$
as the canonical mass lattice base.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\sloppy
Verified computationally for all Farey tower primes $p \leq 281$;
see \S16 of \cite{Gentry:arith}.
\fussy\end{proof}

\subsection{Open questions}

Two structural questions remain open:
\begin{enumerate}
\item What is the degree-8 number field containing
  $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(\mathtt{AAAAB}))$?
  Its polynomial
  $18x^8 + 243x^7 + 970x^6 + 872x^5 - 729x^4 - 1878x^3 + 37x^2
  - 1659x + 3202$
  has signature $(0,4)$ (totally complex); its relation to the
  Galois closure of $\Kten$ is unknown.
\item Do the specific Hecke eigenvalues $a_p(X_0(11))$ at the
  automorphic level $283 \cdot 31$ encode the fermion mass values
  (not just the lattice base $\varphi$)?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

We have shown that the compact hyperbolic 3-manifold
$\MCKM = m006(-5,2)$ is, among the 134 elements of the SnapPy census with
$H_1 = \Zfive$, the unique one with ITF signature $(8,1)$.
This arithmetic selection criterion identifies $\MCKM$ without fitting.

Two proved structural theorems (the ITF generator identity and the trace
quotient count) and one high-precision numerical result confirmed to
$\sim 10^{-16}$ but not yet an exact algebraic proof (the Fricke
codimension-1 collapse, Observation~\ref{thm:fricke}; see
Remark~\ref{rem:q001status} for the current, partially-resolved status of
that open problem) together show that the CKM optimization navigates a
finite 122-node graph rather than a continuous word space.

The Gaussian overlap construction with $\sigma = 0.47$
(empirically optimal, using the absolute-value kernel
$\theta_{ij} = \arccos|\hat{n}_i \cdot \hat{n}_j|$)
achieves fitness $\mathcal{F} = 0.002728$ against PDG~2024 CKM values
for the optimal triple $(\mathtt{aaab}, \mathtt{aabb}, \mathtt{bAbAB})$.
A null test gives $p = 0/200 < 0.005$, with the null minimum
$0.01131$ lying $4\times$ above the CKM fitness, and this result survives
essentially unchanged under a far stronger, uniform search (best null
still $\sim 2.1\times$ worse than $\MCKM$'s fitness).
A complete census comparison across all 134 $H_1 = \Zfive$ manifolds shows
$\MCKM$ ranking 82nd of 134 --- below the census mean --- and that fitting
the real CKM matrix this well is a generic property of the $H_1 = \Zfive$
torsion class as a whole (Observation~\ref{obs:generic_fitness}), through
the torsion-set smearing scale $\sigma = 2/|H_1|$
(Remark~\ref{rem:torsion_generic}), not a special property of $\MCKM$'s
specific arithmetic. The arithmetic selection criterion and the fitness
result are independent facts; we present them as such rather than
claiming one explains the other.

The Jarlskog invariant $J = 0$ at leading order follows exactly from
the real-valued kernel construction (proved).
The quark/lepton CP asymmetry---$J_\mathrm{CKM} \approx 3 \times 10^{-5}$
vs $J_\mathrm{PMNS} \approx 0.033$---is explained geometrically
by the contrast in ITF signatures: $(8,1)$ (predominantly real) for
the quark sector vs $(0,1)$ (imaginary quadratic) for the lepton sector.
